# A Drill counts as one review per Concept

Drill composition gives a weak or newly-studied Concept two Questions (a recall and a flashcard), so letting every Attempt advance the review schedule — the literal reading of the drill ticket — applied SM-2's interval multiplier twice in one sitting. That pushed the weakest Concepts' next review furthest out, the opposite of what drilling them harder is for: a newly-studied Concept answered well twice jumped to a 9-day interval, where a stronger Concept asked once moved 6 days to 17. We considered aggregating the Drill's Attempts and applying one update at the end, but a Drill abandoned half way would then advance nothing. We chose to advance the schedule on the **first** Attempt on each Concept within a Drill and leave later Attempts on that same Concept in the same Drill recorded and graded but non-advancing. For a weak Concept the first Attempt is the recall Question, the strictest test in the mix, so the schedule follows the harder evidence. This refines ADR 0002 rather than replacing it: Correctness and Confidence still drive the interval and ease factor exactly as before. It is hard to reverse for the same reason ADR 0002 is — accumulated review state is computed under this rule, and changing it later would misread every interval already stored.
