# Free-text answers are graded in the background; feedback fills in when ready

Submitting a recall or scenario answer used to wait on the LLM grade before responding: about 7.8 s of grading plus database round trips and the redirect, some 9.3 s in all, spent looking at a spinner. ADR 0010 made grading itself faster; this changes when the learner has to wait for it. It refines how ADR 0003's grading is delivered, not whether answers are LLM-graded.

The attempts route now records the Attempt as **grading pending** and redirects to its feedback at once. It grades in `after()` from `next/server`, which runs once the response has gone, within the route's `maxDuration`. An Attempt carries a grading status, `pending`, `graded` or `failed`; its Correctness, explanation and strong answer are null until it's graded. The feedback screen shows the learner's answer straight away, with **Feedback** and **A strong answer** saying "Grading…". It polls a small read-only endpoint for the status and refreshes once the grade is in. "Next question" works throughout. If grading fails the Attempt is `failed`: the screen says so and offers **Retry grading**, which puts it back to pending and grades it again in the same way. Flashcards are graded mechanically as before, at submission.

Scheduling keeps ADR 0006's rule; only the timing of the update moves. Which Concepts an Attempt advances is still decided at submission, before the Attempt is recorded, and stored on it (`advances_concept_ids`), so the grade, however late and whether first time or on retry, applies exactly that decision. A pending or failed Attempt counts as the Drill's signal for its Concepts just as a graded one would, so a later Attempt can't claim the update. The schedule is advanced only when a grade is stored, and the store only succeeds for an Attempt that is still pending, so two gradings racing on one Attempt can't advance it twice. A failed Attempt advances nothing until a retry succeeds; a learner who never retries leaves those Concepts unadvanced by that Drill, the same cost ADR 0006 accepts for an abandoned Drill.

In a Drill a pending Attempt counts as answered, so the next Question is available. The progress bar shows it as grading, and the results screen counts only graded Attempts until the rest arrive, filling them in the same way.

We considered streaming the grade into the response instead, which avoids storing a pending state but still ties the learner to the request and gives nothing to come back to if they navigate away. We also considered a job queue; `after()` needs no new infrastructure for a single-user app, at the cost that an invocation killed mid-grade leaves its Attempt pending with nothing left to grade it. So an Attempt records when its grading started, and one still pending past the grading time limit (the routes' `maxDuration`, five minutes, well beyond a normal grade) is treated as failed: the screen offers the same retry, which restarts the clock.
